                                                                            PROGRESSIONS

                                                                     R. DE LA BRETÈCHE AND D. FIORILLI

                                          Abstract. We establish unconditional -results for all weighted even moments of primes
                                          in arithmetic progressions. We also study the moments of these moments and establish
                                          lower bounds under GRH. Finally, under GRH and LI we prove an asymptotic for all
                                          moments of the associated limiting distribution, which in turn indicates that our uncon-
                                          ditional and GRH results are essentially best possible. Using our probabilistic results,
                                          we formulate a conjecture on the moments with a precise associated range of validity,
                                          which we believe is also best possible. This last conjecture implies a q-analogue of the
                                          Montgomery-Soundararajan conjecture on the Gaussian distribution of primes in short
                                          intervals. The ideas in our proofs include a novel application of positivity in the ex-
                                          plicit formula and the combinatorics of arrays of characters which are fixed by certain
                                          involutions.

   Much less is known about higher moments of arithmetical sequences in residue classes,

with a notable family of exceptions. Indeed, in the influential work [36], Fouvry, Ganguly,

Kowalski and Michel have computed all moments of the classical divisor function in a

certain range. This work has inspired further developments including generalizations to

coefficients of GL(N) automorphic forms [69, 73, 106, 113, 118]. Moreover, very recently

Nunes has established upper bounds on higher moments of squarefree integers [87]. As for

the sequence (n) (the von Mangoldt function), the third moment has been computed by

Hooley [54], as well as by Vaughan [112] with a major arcs approximation. Hooley has also

formulated a conjecture on higher moments [53] on average over q Q in the restricted

range x/(log x)A Q = o(x/ log x). Other than this conjecture, we are not aware of any

results on higher moments of (n) in arithmetic progressions.

   In this paper, we put forward a new technique which will allow us, through positivity

and involved combinatorics, to establish lower bounds on average for all weighted moments

of (n) in arithmetic progressions. More precisely, we will establish lower bounds on all

moments of moments. The conceptual idea of studying moments of moments has its

roots in Cramer's result [28] on the variance of (x) - x, Hooley's bound [52] on the

t-average of the variance of (et; q, a), Vaughan's upper bound [110] on the higher q-

moments of the variance of (x; q, a), the second author's work [33] on the moments of

the limiting distribution of the variance, as well as the very recent work of Assiotis, Bailey

and Keating [4-7] on moments of moments in the context of families of L-functions and of

characteristic polynomials of large random matrices. The approach in the present paper

is to study the higher moments of (et; q, a) via its t-moments for a given modulus q; in

other words, we consider a double moment, over a (mod q) and over t T . As a side result,

we are also able to study the fixed residue class a = 1, once more on average over t T .

Note also that all our results apply to individual moduli q, rather than to the more usual

average over q.

   The moments of (n) are strongly linked with the spacing properties of zeros of L-

functions (see for instance [22, 28, 39]), and a fortiori to the Diophantine properties of

imaginary parts of zeros of L(s, ). An analogous relation was uncovered in the Monach­

number theorem [83, p.484], which depends on an effective version of the linear indepen-
